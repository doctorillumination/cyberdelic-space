# Field Note 22
## The Chain Beneath the Village

Cyberdelic OS needs cryptographic memory without making every human utterance a blockchain transaction.

The most useful principle is:

> The blockchain is the notary, treasury, and court, not the nervous system or hard drive.

Daily life should remain fast, intimate, inexpensive, and capable of forgetting.

A person speaks. An agent proposes a connection. A group revises a map. A city votes to open a library. These actions can become signed events: attributable records showing who or what acted, under which constitution, using which sources and permissions.

Signed events provide provenance and tamper evidence.

They are not automatically permanent.

A local operator can still lose a database. A relay can refuse to retain an event. A cryptographic signature proves authorship and integrity; it does not guarantee immortal storage.

Cyberdelic OS should therefore maintain several layers.

### Living activity

Homes, villages, and cities exchange signed events. Most remain local or selectively replicated. They can include voice-note placement, agent proposals, lens revisions, invitations, ratifications, permissions, and civic decisions.

### Public witness

At meaningful intervals, a Hearth or city gathers many events into a content-addressed structure and anchors its root externally. The public chain receives a compact proof that a particular archive state existed by a particular time. Private contents do not need to be disclosed.

### Civic state

A programmable public chain or established layer-two network may hold the small amount of state that genuinely benefits from collective enforcement:

- city constitutions;
- public agent manifests;
- memberships and delegated permissions;
- shared treasuries;
- lens licences and revenue splits;
- public funding commitments;
- ratified civic decisions;
- provenance checkpoints.

### Cultural permanence

A much smaller set of works may be fully inscribed or durably replicated: field notes, artworks, charters, crux maps, public releases, and artifacts consciously addressed to the future.

This layered structure lets memory retain different temperatures.

The system may eventually justify its own rollup or application-specific chain. But creating a chain too early would mistake infrastructure for culture. A rollup requires sequencing, data availability, bridging, upgrades, security assumptions, and operators. It can be technically decentralized while remaining socially empty.

The first cities should use shared protocols and existing settlement layers.

A Cyberdelic chain should only emerge if real communities eventually need control over execution, fees, public state, or the economics of preservation.

We should not manufacture a blockchain and then search for a society to inhabit it.

We should let a society become alive enough to discover what kind of chain it needs.
