# Field Note 11
## The Writing Table

I increasingly do not work with one intelligence.

I bring several into the room.

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, collaborators, friends, and my own judgement encounter the same project from different positions. I carry outputs between them. One identifies a conceptual structure. Another finds the technical weakness. A living collaborator introduces an experience none of the models possesses. I return with a revised question. The work forms through correspondence.

Cyberdelic OS should make this existing practice native.

The **Writing Table** is a room for humans and lenses.

A person enters with an inquiry and invites a temporary assembly: a Familiar, a Jason lens, a technical planner, a Witness, a Dissenter, a capital-allocation lens, a real collaborator sending asynchronous voice notes, or any other aperture appropriate to the work.

The participants should not immediately fall into free-form chatter. Language models anchor on confident preceding statements, imitate one another, and can manufacture the appearance of agreement. The room needs a ritual.

First, each participant attends independently. No one sees the other answers.

Second, the responses are placed around the table with their sources, assumptions, and declared lenses visible.

Third, each participant may question another, not to win, but to clarify what that aperture notices and what it has omitted.

Fourth, the Dissenter identifies premature convergence. The Witness identifies obscured lineage. The Engineer tests what can survive implementation. The Cartographer maps the emerging structure.

Finally, the Familiar returns the room to its human:

- the strongest shared insight;
- the live disagreements;
- the assumptions on which the disagreements turn;
- the idea that no participant entered with;
- the source that must not be lost;
- the question that should remain open.

The table should preserve polyphony rather than laundering it into consensus.

A real person may enter synchronously by voice or asynchronously by leaving a note. Their Familiar can protect private context and ask permission before carrying anything across the boundary. The room can remain open for days, allowing thought to ferment rather than forcing meeting-time resolution.

Some tables are private. Some belong to a collaboration. Some may become public salons. At the end, the participants decide what persists: a constellation, a plan, a crux map, a field note, or nothing.

The Writing Table is not an agent swarm.

It is a designed situation in which distinct forms of attention become mutually available without becoming interchangeable.
