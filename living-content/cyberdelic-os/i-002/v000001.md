# Instrument I-02: The Library Aperture

- **Project:** CyberdelicOS
- **Type:** Finite curatorial instrument
- **Status:** Living, testing
- **Version:** 0.1
- **Date:** August 16, 2026
- **Primary verbs:** ATTEND, END
- **Supporting verbs:** CARRY, TEND
- **First implementation:** [cyberdelic.space](https://cyberdelic.space)
- **Source:** [doctorillumination/cyberdelic-space](https://github.com/doctorillumination/cyberdelic-space)

*The Library Aperture turns three chosen words into three possible encounters, then releases the reader.*

The instrument begins with a question:

> What are you trying to become capable of seeing?

It does not begin with a trending work, a notification, a personalized recommendation, or an empty search box. It asks the reader to declare a small chord of intention.

Three words are selected from a changing public vocabulary. Three works come into view. The instrument ends.

## What the instrument is

The Library Aperture is a reusable interaction pattern for finite discovery across a shared collection. It can be implemented as a web field, a voice exchange, a physical table, a projected constellation, or another interface, provided it preserves the same constitutional shape:

1. Begin with the reader's intention.
2. Make the available vocabulary visible.
3. Let the reader choose exactly three signals.
4. Compose exactly three encounters from a common catalog.
5. Carry enough context to make each placement inspectable.
6. Stop without opening an automatic next sequence.

The number three is compositional, not metaphysical. One result can feel authoritative. Two can collapse into opposition. Three can form a small field: agreement, tension, and an oblique door. It remains few enough to encounter without becoming a queue.

## What the instrument refuses

- Infinite scroll.
- Behavioral profiling.
- Popularity as the default measure of relevance.
- Engagement optimization.
- A private catalog silently rearranged for each reader.
- Autoplay into another work.
- Notifications designed to produce return.
- The claim that a ranked result is neutral or personally optimal.

The instrument may use an algorithm, but that algorithm must be bounded, inspectable, and answerable to the collection's public metadata. It cannot treat a reader's hesitation, dwell time, pointer movement, or prior visits as material for covert ranking.

## Inputs

The input vocabulary may contain several kinds of terms:

- **Form:** what kind of object a work is.
- **Theme:** what a work attends to.
- **Register:** how a work speaks or behaves.
- **World:** which project or shared context carries it.
- **Era:** when a durable work entered public history.

Terms should be written for human recognition, not only for taxonomic purity. The vocabulary is itself a curatorial aperture and must remain versioned. Adding a word changes what kinds of intention a reader can declare.

## Outputs

Each of the three encounters should carry:

- a stable title and route;
- an honest publication state, such as living, testing, ratified, or inscribed;
- a short description written for encounter rather than promotion;
- the chosen terms that brought it near;
- enough provenance to distinguish editorial placement from factual evidence.

The output language matters. The first implementation says, “These came into view.” It does not say, “Recommended for you,” “You may also like,” or “Top picks.” The instrument describes an encounter without pretending to know the reader.

## Shared sky, situated path

Every reader enters the same public catalog and vocabulary. Different choices produce different constellations, but the underlying works are not privately substituted. This creates a modest form of shared reality: several apertures can approach the same objects through different declared intentions and compare what appeared.

The URL fragment can carry a chord between readers. A shared chord is not a personal profile. It is a temporary, explicit placement that another person can inspect or refuse.

## The automatic path

“Choose for me” is allowed only as a visible, bounded alternative. In the first implementation it uses a deterministic daily rotation, not randomness, popularity, or behavioral data. Everyone receives the same automatic path on the same day.

This preserves surprise without manufacturing a private oracle. It also makes the automatic choice contestable because its cadence and source are public.

## The ending

Ending is the instrument's central output.

After the three works arrive, no additional work is loaded beneath them. The reader can open one, leave, or consciously begin another chord. The system does not convert completion into another opportunity to capture attention.

The ending is not a blank caused by missing inventory. It is a boundary produced on purpose.

## Accessibility contract

Any implementation of the Library Aperture should preserve:

- semantic controls for every choice;
- a fully operable keyboard path;
- visible focus and selected state;
- a textual alternative to every visual relation;
- reduced-motion behavior;
- result announcements that do not overwhelm assistive technology;
- a non-JavaScript or non-visual route to the complete collection;
- touch targets large enough for imprecise input;
- no color-only distinction between selected and unselected words.

The moving field is an atmosphere around the instrument. It is never the only way to operate it.

## Measures of success

The instrument should not be judged by session length, return frequency, cards viewed, or total clicks. More appropriate observations are:

- Did the reader understand why the three works appeared?
- Did one work become newly perceptible?
- Did the finite set feel sufficient rather than abruptly withheld?
- Could the reader leave without being pursued by the interface?
- Did two people using the same chord receive a common object for conversation?
- Did the instrument surface living and inscribed memory without confusing them?
- Did the person leave with more attention than they brought to the screen?

These are partly qualitative questions. Refusing extractive metrics means accepting that not every success should be remotely measured.

## Possible embodiments

- A museum table where three physical tokens reveal three works.
- A voice interface that asks for three words and reads three short invitations.
- A classroom instrument where several students compare constellations from one shared chord.
- A local Hearth view that composes from a private archive without exporting its contents.
- A correspondence ritual in which two people exchange chords rather than links.
- A public library kiosk that resets completely after each encounter.

Each embodiment may change the surface. None should remove the ending.

## Questions still open

- Is three the right constant for every context, or should some embodiments use another declared finite number?
- How much explanation helps a reader without turning the result into a technical audit?
- Should one result be intentionally distant from the chosen chord?
- Can collective chords be formed without converting group attention into voting or popularity?
- How should vocabulary disputes be recorded when a work's placement is contested?
- Can an instrument invite return without manufacturing compulsion?
