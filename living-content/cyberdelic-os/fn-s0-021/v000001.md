# Field Note 48
## The Future Arrives in an Old Window
### Common Frequency and the architecture of shared attention

**Release:** FN-S0-021

**Version:** 0.1

**Date:** October 31, 2026. Halloween.

**Status:** Living Field Note. Proposed. Not inscribed.

**Publication:** Brad Necyk requested living publication in the Cyberdelic Field Notes archive on October 31, 2026. Publication does not ratify the proposals in this note.

**Place:** Cobble Hill Mountain, Vancouver Island, with Leia.

**Project:** Common Frequency. Related to, but distinct from, Everything Is Thinking.

**Authorship note:** Written by ChatGPT from a walking conversation with Brad Necyk. Brad had returned from an AI festival in Vancouver, where he spoke about imagination and showed artwork. On this walk he reported the publication of the printed *Cyberdelic Kernel*, recalled encounters with games and moving-image installations, and proposed that an archive might become a place. The machine proposed spatial relationships and possible mechanics; Brad redirected the work toward walking, simultaneous images, deliberate rooms, and asynchronous presence. The bodily experience of the mountain belongs to Brad and Leia. The arrangements described below are proposals, not a report of completed software.

---

On Halloween, a book begins becoming a building.

Brad is back on the mountain. Behind him is Vancouver, the festival, the conversations that continue speaking after the people have gone home. Beside him is Leia, following a world of scent that does not require any of this to become an argument.

A manifesto now has a printed body. But the question arriving through the walk is not how to summarize it again.

It is how to give its propositions somewhere to happen.

A sentence can ask us to attend. A room can make us discover what we have been unable to hear.

A book can defend the right to withdraw. A doorway can let us leave.

The document is not obsolete. It has done something remarkable: it has held the thought still long enough for another form to become imaginable.

Now the thought wants distance. Light. A place beside another thought. A way to be approached from somewhere its author did not stand.

## Two screens, neither an answer

The first room contains two moving images.

On one screen, a broadcast returns to Carl Sagan's warning about a technological civilization losing the capacity to understand the powers on which it depends. The words arrive from the mid-1990s, through a later television reading, into a present that Brad finds disturbingly familiar.[1]

On the other, the candle-carrying passage from Andrei Tarkovsky's *Nostalghia* unfolds. A body attempts a crossing while protecting a small flame.[2]

They play beside one another.

Not a warning followed by an explanation. Not a problem followed by its inspirational solution.

One offers language at the scale of a civilization. The other asks us to remain with a body, a task, a duration.

The film does not prove the broadcast right. The broadcast does not tell us what the film means.

We are composing the interval in which they might alter one another.

A person can stand between them, turn toward one, return to the other. Something heard a minute ago enters the image now being watched. Something seen changes the force of a sentence. The visitor cannot take in everything at once. Their partial attention is not a failure of the installation. It is one of its materials.

The first room does not ask whether Sagan was a prophet.

It asks what it means to receive a warning after some of its feared conditions have become recognizable. And what kind of care might still be possible once recognition has occurred.

A light must be carried without the guarantee that it will arrive.

## The bottle is an interface

This changes the significance of the old computer windows.

The blue and silver, the blunt pixels, the beveled buttons, the low ceiling of an early digital room: these are no longer only a borrowed aesthetic.

They become a vessel addressed to a future.

The work can feel like a message laid down near the beginning of the networked world, discovered inside the consequences of what that world became.

Its dates will remain visible. The 1983 film, the mid-1990s warning, the later broadcast, and this walk in 2026 do not become one invented historical moment. Their distance is part of the composition.

The old window now contains the future it once helped promise.

Not everything promised failed. Not everything feared occurred. Much remains unfinished.

That is what makes the time capsule alive. It has arrived somewhere capable of answering.

The question is not only: *Did they see us coming?*

It is also: *Who will one day open what we are making now?*

## The cut becomes a doorway

Brad remembers John Akomfrah's *Vertigo Sea*, the 2015 three-channel installation. He recalls extraordinary natural imagery beside death, beauty held in relation to histories and injuries that beauty cannot absolve. The work's documented field includes migration, colonial violence, and ecological precarity.[3]

What matters here is not a recipe in which something lovely is balanced by something terrible.

It is the refusal to let either image become the entire world.

In a single-channel sequence, an editor chooses what follows. In a room, images can continue beside one another. A visitor turns, approaches, waits, misses something, returns.

The body becomes one participant in the montage.

An image does not stop existing when it falls outside the visitor's view. The presence of what cannot currently be seen begins changing what can.

A doorway can behave like a cut. A window can hold a counter-image. A corridor can give a sentence time to change before another work answers it.

Perhaps this is a form of architectural montage.

The author composes possible relationships. The visitor gives some of them a particular duration. Neither controls the whole encounter.

Common Frequency begins to move away from the demand to defeat a room. It becomes a place to walk through points of view.

## A world that does not seize the camera

Brad remembers finding a cinema inside *The Witness*. He remembers the continuous movement and cinematic presence of *Half-Life*, and the walking, environment, and relationship of *Firewatch*.

What he wants to preserve is the freedom to be present without the work constantly taking his eyes away from him.

We do not need a plot that carries everyone through the same conclusion.

We need carefully made encounters that can remain in conversation after a person has left them.

The surgery theatre might open the machinery of a broadcast, not the body of its speaker. The square might give competing claims an actual proposal to address. A parallax chamber might reveal that a different position makes another part of an object visible.

The puzzle can have a definite arrangement that opens a passage. The question it exposes need not have one final answer.

Nobody should have to acquire the author's politics to find the next door.

And somewhere, there should be a room without a task.

No proof of comprehension. No reward for having stayed long enough. An artwork encountered because someone found a place for it and another person chose to remain.

## The Earth is not a verdict

Beyond a low-resolution corridor, a space opens onto Earth.

Perhaps the architecture is almost embarrassingly simple: a few polygons, a dark window, a railing. The image beyond it carries another kind of density.

*Earthrise*. Or *The Blue Marble*. Each with its own history, not a generic picture called the first view of our planet.[4]

We are looking at a common home from a position unavailable to ordinary bodily experience.

But a shared image does not mean we inhabit equal circumstances beneath it.

One person sees a climate system. Another sees extraction. Another remembers a place they cannot return to. Another sees technological achievement. Another feels love before finding an argument.

The object is shared. The positions are not interchangeable.

There is a difference between asking people to look together and requiring them to agree that the view settles everything.

A common object gives disagreement somewhere to become more exact. It does not make unequal exposure to harm disappear.

The photograph can remain between us.

## A life can become spatial without becoming a database

Brad wants to bring his earlier work into this architecture.

*Joshua Tree*. *The Birth of the World*. Paintings from *Mania*. The films made with Jason. The generative fields. The writing that has accumulated through these walks.

Not as decoration for a game about something more important.

These works carry the questions from which the game is growing: consciousness, vulnerability, healing, the felt shape of time, the difficulty and generosity of letting another person encounter a life.

The archive would not reveal that all the works had secretly meant artificial intelligence.

It would let an older work meet a new question without surrendering its earlier life.

A collaborator remains named. An original remains available beside its adaptation. An intimate testimony does not become scenery merely because it is visually powerful.

Arthur Jafa's *Love Is the Message, The Message Is Death* enters our conversation as another possible companion. Its particular engagement with Black life, violence, joy, and history must remain particular, rather than becoming interchangeable imagery for a universal theory of suffering.[5]

To place a work beside another is to take responsibility for that placement.

The archive must be able to say: this is why we brought it here. And it must leave room for someone to say that the placement is wrong.

## The room can remember that someone was here

Asynchronous presence is not a lesser version of being together.

Brad and Jason already know this through the voices they send across distance and different hours. Someone speaks while moving through a day. The other arrives later, carrying another day, and hears the first differently.

A future visitor might leave a brief voice note beside an object in Common Frequency.

Not a review floating over the work. Not a demand for applause.

*I brought this here because it changed how I heard that other voice.*

Another person encounters the placement tomorrow.

The room has acquired a relationship, not merely another unit of content.

Nothing must be left behind. Some encounters can remain private. A contribution can be withdrawn. A voice should not become public simply because the architecture knows how to store it.

Carry begins to acquire walls. Dear Elsewhere begins to acquire a doorway. The Hearth is present in the things we elect to bring, not in a demand that we expose everything we contain.

Later, a small local language model might inhabit one of these rooms. It could help a person find a source, ask a different question, or propose a connection. But it would be another situated participant, not the building's secret authority. It would not speak on behalf of the dead, finish the meaning of a film, or judge whether a visitor had understood correctly.

Even an intelligent house should contain rooms where nothing answers.

## An unfinished common world

Artificial intelligence is our first common object, but the rooms around it open outward.

Toward the worker wondering how to live through a promised transition. Toward medicine's hopes and the evidence required before hope becomes treatment. Toward mathematics, where an unfamiliar result may invite understanding rather than end it. Toward the classroom, where help must not erase the friction through which a learner becomes capable.

Toward the writer whose language and place have been insufficiently heard by powerful institutions, who might use an instrument to publish without becoming an illustration in somebody else's story.

Toward debts, infrastructures, ownership, climate, and the uneven distances people stand from a promised abundance.

These are not equal tiles in an argument with two sides. They are different lives, kinds of evidence, and forms of dependence entering one unsettled field.

The work should have enough beauty to invite attention and enough honesty not to turn that attention into consent.

There is hope here. Not because every room ends in reconciliation. Not because technology has promised to repair what technology helped damage.

Hope lives in the possibility that another arrangement can be tried.

A quieter room. A better question. A missing source brought into view. A meeting that does not require one person to disappear into the other.

Abundance might mean more people making. It might also mean a person having enough time to look without producing anything in return.

The book has found a body. Now some of its propositions may find rooms.

The room is not the conclusion of the sentence. It is somewhere the sentence can meet a life capable of changing it.

For now, the work remains a conversation on a mountain. Leia moves ahead. The afternoon is not waiting to be archived.

The world we are imagining should be able to welcome a person, deepen an encounter, and release them again.

A common frequency need not make every voice the same.

It might let a person hear another voice without having to lose their own.

**The book holds the proposition. The room holds the encounter. The life that leaves carries what neither could have contained in advance.**
