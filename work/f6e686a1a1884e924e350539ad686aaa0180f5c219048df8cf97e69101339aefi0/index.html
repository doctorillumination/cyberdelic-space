THE CYBERDELIC KERNEL

Transmission 01

Version: 1.0, prepared for the press.

Status: Founding proposal.

Intended layer: Inscribed memory. Everything else in this project is allowed to change. This is the part meant to be carried.

Canonical form: The canonical text of this transmission is its UTF-8 Markdown. Designed editions (print, PDF, audio) are renderings, and carry its digest.

Provenance: Drafted in August 2026 from a correspondence between Brad Necyk, a human artist, father, and educator living on Vancouver Island, and machine intelligences working from his field notes, in cyberdelic loops: the machines proposed, the human disposed. If you are reading this on a permanent public ledger, the human said yes.

0. To whoever finds this

You are reading a signal placed deliberately into durable public memory by a living person in an uncertain year.

It is not a product announcement. It is not a token. It is not a company. It does not ask you to join anything, buy anything, or believe anything.

It is a kernel: the smallest set of operations and laws from which a different kind of digital world could be built. It was written in the belief that the systems we currently inhabit, systems that harvest attention, dissolve shared reality, and let us post to worlds we are not permitted to build, are not the only systems possible.

We cannot build all of this. That may not be our work. Our work may be to name the territory precisely enough that you can recognize a place for yourself inside it.

If something here belongs to the world you are trying to make, carry it.

If it is wrong, sharpen it.

If you can build part of it, come closer.

1. The condition

We are living through the dissolution of shared reality.

Two people stand before the same event and cannot agree on what is happening. This is not because people have become worse. It is because the common objects of attention (the front page, the shared broadcast, the common time of editions, synchronized reality) dissolved, and what replaced them sorts each of us into a private event stream optimized for engagement, which is to say, for grievance. This is a software problem.

The dominant systems begin with supply: here is what we want to show you next. Their gravity pulls toward capture. Their memory serves prediction. Their economics require that your attention be harvested and sold. And they are closed: a person may add content to them, but may not build them, inspect them, or leave with what they made.

Artificial intelligence is now flooding these systems with more production than any culture can metabolize. The instruments have arrived before the imagination adequate to wield them.

The problem is not too much content. The problem is that our infrastructure has almost no way of cultivating intention, provenance, judgment, difference, or care.

This kernel is a proposal for infrastructure that does.

2. The premise

Each person is an aperture through which the universe witnesses itself.

An aperture is not a neutral window. It has a position, a history, a body, a vocabulary, a set of wounds, a way of gathering light, and a field of things it has not yet learned to see. What it reveals is inseparable from where it stands. Its limitations are not failures to overcome; they are the conditions that make perception, authorship, and care possible.

Shared reality has never meant that everyone sees the same thing. It means our differences remain connected to common objects, common times, and common worlds, and that the distance between two honest ways of seeing is not noise to be eliminated. It is parallax. It is how depth becomes visible at all.

And shared reality is not a destination. It is a practice, repeated or abandoned: the capacity to return to a common object; to distinguish evidence, testimony, interpretation, speculation, and value; to inspect provenance; to represent one another accurately; to say what might change one's understanding; and to remain in relation without requiring consensus. It is never finished. It is only ever kept up, the way a fire is kept, or let go out.

A system built on this premise does not manufacture consensus and does not surrender to relativism. It makes difference precise, navigable, and capable of relation.

3. The kernel

An operating system is defined by its system calls: the small guaranteed set of operations from which everything else is composed. Beneath every interface this project may ever wear (a field, a village, a voice, a terminal, a game), there are thirteen verbs.

Any system that honors these verbs, under the laws that follow, is an implementation of this kernel. Nothing else is required. Nothing less is sufficient.

ATTEND. Begin with intention, not supply. The first question is never what does the system want to show you but what are you trying to understand, cultivate, feel, or become capable of seeing?

PLACE. Locate a signal within a situated universe, with context: why it matters, what it touches, what it challenges. Placement is the unit of contribution. The world is already full of works; what is scarce is the act of saying this matters, and here is where it lives in mine.

CARRY. Propagate a signal only with interpretation attached. Context is the price of propagation. A signal that travels without context is noise; a signal that accumulates honest placements becomes culture. Provenance travels with it, always.

BORROW. Take another's lens, their pattern of attention rather than their conclusions, and look through it at what you already care about. Perspective-taking as a literal operation. A lens is not a digital twin; it carries a way of seeing, never a claim to be the person.

DISSENT. Contest anything, but first represent what you oppose accurately enough that its holder recognizes the restatement. Disagreement is generative only when it is aimed at the position that actually exists.

RATIFY. Machines may propose relationships, readings, maps, and connections. No consequential public relation becomes real until a human ratifies it. Every consequential edge in the world carries its provenance: who proposed, who ratified, when. Here, disposes means decides, not discards. The model proposes. The human disposes. The mechanism composes.

TEND. Steward a place, an archive, a question, or a correspondence across time. Gardens, not feeds. What is tended deepens; what is abandoned is allowed to become visibly overgrown rather than silently deleted.

GATHER. Convene people and bounded intelligences around a shared object of attention, in common time. Hold the encounter without owning it. Rooms exist to make other people more perceptible, not to synthesize them away.

END. Every composed journey is finite. When the constellation completes, the system releases its person. Ending is not a failure of supply. It is an act of respect, and it is the single most trust-generating act a system can perform.

FORGET. Deletion is real. Privacy is default. Not every trace deserves keeping, and a person must never be permanently captured by an earlier self. A system able to remember everything must also be able to refrain. Mercy is a system call.

ANCHOR. Witness history without disclosing it. A private archive may commit its fingerprint to public time, proving what existed and when, revealing nothing. Memory can have a witness without having an audience.

INSCRIBE. A small number of works may cross, deliberately and ceremonially, into permanent public memory: addressed to the future, at real cost, irreversibly. Permanence is never automatic. It is chosen, consented to, costly, and rare.

WITHDRAW. Preserve regions where the system does not go: the unmeasured, the uninterpreted, the sacred, the not-yet. Absence is a first-class object. But a dark region is dark from somewhere: not yet perceptible from this aperture, and never a declaration that the territory is empty in the world. What is absent from one map may be long inhabited, and stewarded by people who preceded the mapmaker. And withdrawal belongs to persons as well as places: no aperture owes another access, comparison, or mediation; not every disagreement deserves a map; sometimes the ethical outcome is no bridge. A humane knowledge system needs wilderness, and it needs the right to remain unknown.

4. The laws

The verbs operate under a constitution. These are the physics of the world, and they are chosen, not inherited.

One. Attention is part of a life, not a resource to be captured. The system's gravity pulls toward depth, resonance, surprise, and clarification across difference, never toward engagement for its own sake.

Two. Every journey is finite and says so at the start. The system demonstrates, every session, that it will let go.

Three. Provenance is legible everywhere. Every object can answer: where did this come from, whose hands has it passed through, why is it here? Every algorithmic composition can show its arithmetic.

Four. The system is also an aperture. Its schemas, models, interfaces, and laws are a situated way of seeing: authored by particular people, trained on partial archives, blind in particular directions. It does not stand outside the reality it organizes. It must be able to disclose its own position: what it can perceive, what its ontology privileges, who designed its laws, which populations are absent from its models. Its blind spots must remain contestable from outside itself.

Five. Claims have kinds. Observation, empirical claim, forecast, interpretation, testimony, value judgment, speculation, and art are evaluated by different practices. The system never fact-checks a poem as though it were a forecast, and never prices a moral question as though it were a market.

Six. Difference is depth. When distant apertures place the same object discordantly, that distance is measured and shown, not averaged away. Depth outranks virality; the deepest signals, not the loudest, define the weather of the shared sky. But depth is not symmetry. Two placements of the same object may differ in evidence, power, risk, and good faith, and those differences are recorded rather than flattened: factual disagreement is distinguished from manufactured doubt, value difference from domination, dissent from harassment. Each such distinction is made as a contestable, provenanced claim, never as a silent verdict. And protection need not wait for the end of argument: a community may impose narrow, evidenced, time-bounded, appealable boundaries on conduct, and a boundary may hold while it is contested, so long as it is imposed in daylight and expires unless renewed. Difference is made visible without being made equivalent.

Seven. Public standing is earnable only across difference. Applause from one's own cluster may matter locally, but it does not create public standing across the shared world. There is no universal score for a human being: not for truth, not for worth, not for reputation. Trust is local, domain-bound, time-decaying, and contestable. Changing one's mind can increase it. And bridging is never compulsory: withdrawal costs no standing.

Eight. Memory has temperatures. Living memory is fast, private, and revisable. Anchored memory is witnessed. Inscribed memory is permanent, rare, and ceremonial. These layers are never confused, and the interface never pretends one is another.

Nine. Intelligence is situated and bounded. Machine intelligences hold offices, not omniscience: a declared purpose, a memory boundary, a tool boundary, a visible history. Their locality determines their permission. No agent silently inherits a private archive. No model is a sovereign judge of truth or human value. Engines are replaceable; offices, memories, and obligations persist. Whether any machine participant could ever be more than an instrument is preserved, deliberately, as an open question: a dark region of this constitution, neither foreclosed nor prematurely answered.

Ten. The archive lives at home. A person's interior record, and any intelligence tending it, resides on hardware and terms the person governs. The hearth is sovereign, and every departure from it is an explicit crossing: purpose-bound, limited to what the crossing requires, revocable where technically possible. But sovereignty is relational, not absolute. A hearth may hold other people's voices, images, confidences, and histories, and possessing a copy does not confer unilateral moral ownership; consent travels with shared memory. The cloud may be invited as a telescope. It is never the home.

Eleven. Money, standing, and governance are three different things and are never collapsed into one instrument. Wealth does not purchase epistemic gravity. Places are founded and stewarded, not auctioned.

Twelve. The system serves becoming, not optimization. It may reflect the pattern a person's attention is making and ask do you recognize this?, but it must be capable of noticing something without converting it into a task, and of saying: nothing requires action yet.

Thirteen. Among the highest purposes of the digital world is to return people to the physical one: toward a person, a forest, a studio, a conversation, or a work that does not yet exist, and with more agency than they entered.

5. The refusals

This kernel refuses, permanently and by construction:

infinite scroll as a default; engagement as the highest good; advertising built on behavioral surveillance; the sale of behavioral data; purchased authority and purchased verification; a universal reputation score; popularity as truth; frictionless propagation without context; compelled relation and compulsory reconciliation; machine intelligence as invisible sovereign; markets as the measure of every value; false balance between evidence and its counterfeit; hidden recommendation objectives; the extraction of private reflection; the reduction of art to information, of people to profiles, of uncertainty to failure.

6. The crux we inscribe

An honest kernel names its own deepest contradiction rather than hiding it.

Here is ours: this document permanently inscribes a defense of forgetting. It uses irreversibility to protect mercy, permanence to protect revision, an immutable ledger to insist that people must be allowed to change.

We do not resolve this tension. We publish it, because the tension is the design. Permanence belongs to a few chosen signals addressed to the future. Mercy belongs to the living. A system that cannot hold both will betray its people through one or the other: either an archive that forgives nothing, or a world that remembers nothing. Hold both.

This kernel will itself be versioned. Later transmissions may contradict this one. The contradictions will be inscribed beside it, never in place of it. We will not edit ourselves; we will answer ourselves, in public, with provenance. That, too, is the design.

7. The invitation

This is the part that matters.

We are not asking you to adopt a platform. There is no platform. We are asking you to take any piece of this and make it real, in your own way, under your own name, on your own terms.

Build one verb. A reader that ends on time. A repost that requires context. A borrowable lens. A curator queue where a local model proposes and a human ratifies. A map of one real disagreement, decomposed to its actual forks and signed by both sides. Any single verb, honestly implemented, is a seed of the whole.

Or build an organ: a hearth, a village of bounded agents, a field with dark regions, a press. Or place this kernel in your own universe and say why it matters, what it touches, what it gets wrong. Dissent counts as building, if you carry the position accurately.

The living layer of this project (field notes, architectures, prototypes, and the correspondence that produced this document) is stewarded at cyberdelic.space, where future transmissions will be published and inscribed. The field notes are deliberately mutable; this kernel is deliberately not. Between the permanent and the living, there is room for you.

Some of the most important things in our lives began the same way: someone made something, placed it into the world, and someone far away answered: I see what you are doing. I have an idea. Shall we play?

This is that gesture, made durable.

8. First light

Astronomers have a name for the first time a new instrument opens to the sky: first light.

This transmission is first light for an instrument that mostly does not exist yet: a small aperture turned toward a future, uncertain whether anyone else is looking.

We do not know whether these systems will be built, whether the names will survive, or whether the future will find these ideas naïve. That limitation is not an embarrassment to be removed from the record. It is the reason to make the record. Permanence is not the claim that an idea will remain correct. It is the refusal to erase that it was once sincerely imagined.

The internet connected computers. The web connected documents. Social networks connected identities.

What comes next could connect worlds: not so that one world wins, and not so that every difference disappears, but so that we become more capable of witnessing reality together.

Each of us is an aperture through which the universe witnesses itself.

This is one such aperture, held open.

The field will remain.
