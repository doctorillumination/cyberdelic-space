What the sources state

The Computational Democracy Project describes Polis as an open-source, real-time system that gathers and analyzes what large groups think in their own words using statistics and machine learning. Participants submit short statements and respond to statements with agree, disagree, or pass. The project's methods paper describes distributed feedback, shared understanding, and cultivating points of consensus.

What the Cartographer infers

Polis and FN-25 share a serious inquiry into large-group sensemaking, but their proximity should not erase their difference. Polis operates through participant statements, voting, opinion-space mapping, and an explicit concern with consensus. FN-25 imagines storms that may never reach consensus and public artifacts that preserve dissent, alternative branches, provenance, and unresolved questions.

This is a proposed relation, not a claim of endorsement, partnership, or methodological equivalence.
