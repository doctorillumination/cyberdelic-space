# Common ground

The basic unit is a mark, not an object with a shaded surface. Each mark has a precise outline, one ink, one density, and a place on the page. The vocabulary begins with circles, crosses, polygons, and repetitions of those parts. A carefully constructed path makes a complex motif as portable as a circle.

Colour lives between the marks. On its own, a piece is bright and open. Where two pieces meet, multiply compositing gives their shared region a deeper colour. Geometry remains legible through the encounter. The implementation fills each motif once, so only separate pieces produce an additional layer of ink.

Motion gives the marks a little resistance and a little memory. A fixed-step spring follows the hand; damping decides how long the gesture lingers. The aim is carefully tuned responsiveness rather than a model of rigid objects. There is no collision force between pieces, because occupying a common place is the central gesture.

The first arrangement is a small specimen study: six encounters, generous paper around them, and a visible vocabulary below. Seeded changes vary the palette and registration. The shape paths and spring system can later belong to a much larger symmetric field without changing the material rules established here.

The second study places the marks in a living field. A seed, cell address, and recipe generation determine each local combination. Density changes the number of cells; spread changes their reach; mark scale changes their contact. Keeping these separate makes growth legible without weakening the original material rules.

Symmetry is an operation on a shared animated source region. Horizontal and vertical reflections produce the mirrored field. Quarter turns produce rotational symmetry. A triangular fold adds diagonal reflection for eightfold symmetry. Compositing the ink before these operations preserves colour and layer order across all echoes, including in ordinary transparent blending.

The motion remains shared through the transformation. Opposite regions have no independent clocks. The default stack is precisely registered: each constituent has the same origin and fixed orientation. Growth begins at that origin, and the breathing envelope belongs to the stack rather than to separate floating layers. Selected stacks exchange neighbouring cells as complete units, settling exactly at the next centre. The earlier angled sway remains an optional Loose mode.

Complexity comes from uneven density. Seeded patches leave blank paper; light cells carry one or two marks; deep stacks may converge into dense coloured discs. A dense field can therefore look elaborate at a distance while revealing a small, elementary vocabulary at close range. Zoom and pan change the camera, while cell count, spread, and mark size remain separate compositional decisions. Vectors are redrawn at the camera scale. Music is not yet an input to this system.
